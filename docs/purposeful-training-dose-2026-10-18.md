# Purposeful training dose and accountable coaching — 18 October 2026

## Problem and resulting behavior

A recent-tolerated ceiling was reduced by the sport template, loss of sessions during scheduling, and a second generic cycle ramp. Uncertain provider copies also triggered a physical-recovery cut. Seven available days did not persist a no-fixed-rest preference.

New plan generation opts into `template-dose-once-v2` allocation. The sport template retains its existing progression/recovery cadence. Scheduling allocates its intended weekly total within the reviewed baseline and daily limits, redistributing only easy familiar time; demanding bouts are never enlarged to fill time. Capacity may remain unallocated when appropriate easy slots do not fit. Existing historical cycles are not rewritten by deploying code.

The separately saved `restPreference` offers experience defaults or no fixed rest. It never changes experience to professional, renews safety confirmation, changes the tolerated baseline or clears training. The narrow revision-checked schedule editor preserves all other setup evidence. A replacement cycle still needs preview and confirmation. No-fixed-rest plans retain easy recovery practice (up to 20 minutes within the existing budget, not a validated universal minimum); insufficient budget, unavailable/performed days, events, symptoms and daily reassessment still override a seven-day schedule. Optional pairs split existing easy time and preserve the source day. Split bouts have honest easy-practice titles rather than retaining a misleading long-session label.

Cycle shaping no longer stacks a second ramp or additional four-week recovery rhythm on an already-periodized template. Event windows and recent difficult/incomplete-work review remain separately reduction-only product policies. The weekly ledger shows scheduled minutes, event/recovery factor and remaining planned minutes; performed/protected days are excluded and never treated as zero work. Potential imported copies retain unknown totals and a history-review flag; uncertain record identity alone no longer means poor physical recovery. No duplicate decisions are invented.

## Coaching behavior

Jas KCoach shows purpose → controlled execution → feedback → reviewed progression. A local, bilingual “push/challenge/motivate me” response connects accountability to current approved work, distinguishes motivation from time or physical recovery, asks a useful question and retains change confirmation. Symptom handling remains earlier in the decision chain. The DeepSeek instruction uses the same approach: firm support without shame, invented capacity, added load to motivate, universal progression percentages or guaranteed daily results. Missing inputs remain missing. No new device/Strava data is exported to an external model by this change.

## Research basis and limits

- [Bourdon et al., 2017 — Monitoring Athlete Training Loads: Consensus Statement](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28463642/): monitoring external work and individual responses informs coaching decisions. This consensus does not validate JMM's allocation percentages.
- [Saw et al., 2016 — athlete training response systematic review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26423706/): regular subjective reports help interpret athlete response alongside other monitoring. Missing/uncertain imported records are not themselves symptoms.
- [Teixeira et al., 2012 — exercise and self-determination theory systematic review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22726453/): autonomous motivation and perceived competence are associated with exercise participation/adherence. Much of the evidence is observational; this does not guarantee elite performance or validate an automated coaching agent.

The software change repairs accounting and scheduling logic. It is not a fitted individual dose-response model, clinical clearance, proof of scientific efficacy or a promise that more minutes produce better outcomes. Weekly baseline remains a reviewed ceiling, not a quota. Clinical/context limitations and sport-specific benchmark provenance still apply.
