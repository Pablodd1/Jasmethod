# JMM training evidence and conditional protocol rules

Evidence checked: 7 October 2026. This is a targeted, source-verified review, not an exhaustive systematic review or a personalized medical/training prescription. Research protocols are examples that were tested, not automatically safe starting doses. Numerical coaching examples below are explicitly marked **inference** when they were not independently validated. Sex counts are reported only when verified. Null findings are not equivalence tests; acute physiological responses are not long-term adaptations.

## Decisions that should change JMM

1. Remove any universal requirement for a weekly double-hard day. Double sessions, double-threshold sessions, two hard days per week, and two maximal sessions on one day are different exposures. Current verified evidence does not establish that everybody should do any of these.
2. Maintain separate sport/event templates: distance running, track acceleration/maximal velocity/speed endurance, cycling, pool swimming, triathlon, and HYROX. A cycling HIIT trial cannot validate the same running, swimming or HYROX dose.
3. Give each prescription a stated goal, relevant population, intensity anchor, dose, recovery, weekly context, progression rule, safety gate, and evidence label. Do not advertise a uniquely optimal protocol from a small short trial.
4. Separate readiness from ambition. A request to push harder can change tone and adherence support; it cannot override pain, illness, technical failure, inadequate recovery, or unverified training history.
5. Use event performance as the primary outcome where possible. VO2max, lactate, economy, and time to exhaustion are distinct outcomes and cannot be translated directly into a promised race-time improvement.

## Authoritative synthesis and best-practice sources

- **Sprint coaching, not an RCT:** Haugen et al. 2019 integrates research and elite practice and explicitly notes that much experimental sprint evidence comes from team sports. It describes acceleration work over 10–50 m with full recovery, and flying maximal-speed work over 10–30 m after individualized run-ins. Beginner/development examples use 20-m accelerations with ~2-min rest or 10-m flying sections with ~4-min rest; elite examples require much longer recovery. End on technical deterioration or performance drop-off. These are expert-practice ranges, not validated universal doses. [Full text; DOI 10.1186/s40798-019-0221-0](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40798-019-0221-0).
- **Elite threshold practice is descriptive:** Haugen and colleagues' 2024 Norwegian training-session study describes elite coaching, including double-threshold practice and approximate 2–4.5 mmol/L lactate targets. Coaches' successful practice does not isolate causality, establish novice safety, or mean every athlete's threshold is at that lactate. [Source; DOI 10.1007/s40279-024-02067-4](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-024-02067-4).
- **Concurrent training:** Schumann et al. 2022 meta-analysis of 43 studies found no clear average impairment of maximal strength or whole-muscle hypertrophy, but explosive strength gains could be attenuated, especially in the same session. Separation of at least 3 h was a subgroup distinction, not a proven exact recovery threshold. [PubMed; DOI 10.1007/s40279-021-01587-7](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34757594/).
- **Taper:** Bosquet et al. 2007 pooled 27 studies; about 2 weeks and 41–60% volume reduction with intensity/frequency maintained was a useful competitive-athlete pattern, not a universal optimum. “Maintain intensity” means retaining some quality at reduced volume, not preserving the entire hard workload. [PubMed; DOI 10.1249/mss.0b013e31806010e0](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17762369/).
- **Load and safety:** IOC 2016 emphasizes individual, flexible external/internal load management including life stress, symptoms and recovery. Its historical numerical ACWR/10% examples should not be encoded as validated safety guarantees. [Consensus; DOI 10.1136/bjsports-2016-096581](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2016-096581). Impellizzeri et al. 2020 explains why causal injury-prevention claims from ACWR are unsupported. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32502973/).
- **Energy availability:** IOC 2023 REDs consensus applies across sexes; problematic under-fueling can compromise health and performance. A chatbot should not diagnose REDs from a score or prescribe aggressive weight loss to improve performance. Concerning symptoms warrant qualified assessment and stopping automatic load escalation. [Consensus; DOI 10.1136/bjsports-2023-106994](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/57/17/1073.long).

## Intensity ontology: prevent zone errors

Use named physiological concepts before zone numbers. A three-zone research model generally means Z1 below first threshold, Z2 between first and second thresholds, and Z3 above second threshold. This is not equivalent to every watch's five-zone model. “Zone 2” in consumer endurance coaching may refer to easy work below the first threshold. Store `zone_model`, `sport`, `anchor_type`, `test_protocol`, `test_date`, `units`, and `confidence` with every target.

- LT1/VT1, LT2/VT2, maximal lactate steady state, critical power/speed and FTP are related constructs, not interchangeable synonyms. “Threshold” alone is too ambiguous for an executable prescription.
- Avoid universal 2 or 4 mmol/L cutoffs as an athlete's true thresholds. Fixed-lactate test outcomes can be reported as such; retain the method used in the original study.
- HR is modality-specific and lags short intervals; heat, fatigue, hydration and medications can change it. Use pace/power plus perceived effort and technique for short intervals; do not chase an instant HR target by sprinting the first repetition.
- Cycling: preserve measured power and device/source. An FTP-percentage workout is a practical convention, not proof the athlete stays in a physiological domain. Recalibrate after changed fitness or discrepant response.
- Running: anchor to recent relevant performance or sport-specific testing; adjust for incline, surface and environmental conditions. Track sprint speed must use measured time/distance and quality, not aerobic HR zones.
- Swimming: CSS from 200/400 m can be calculated as `(400−200)/(t400−t200)` in m/s, or pace per 100 m as `(t400−t200)/2` seconds. Label it an estimate, ensure same stroke/pool/start conventions, and use repeatability and technique. Do not give an inexperienced swimmer an unsupervised maximal test merely to fill a field.
- HYROX: running pace after a station can be slower with high HR; do not label it automatically as the same intensity as fresh running at the same pace. Track station load, repetitions and local fatigue separately.

## Developer-ready conditional rules

The following are implementation proposals, not validated clinical prediction rules. They should be audited by qualified sport professionals before deployment.

### Required fields before selecting demanding protocols

`primary_sport`, `event_distance`, `goal`, `age_group`, `training_status`, `recent_consistent_training`, `recent_weekly_volume_by_sport`, `recent_hard_sessions`, `strength_experience`, `injury_or_illness_flags`, `recovery_constraints`, `equipment_and_safe_setting`, `intensity_anchor_quality`, `competition_date`, `user_wants_doubles`, `coach_supervision`.

Training status is sport-specific. A strong cyclist can be a novice runner; a fast runner can be an inexperienced swimmer or lifter. Do not use ambition, an estimated VO2max, sex, or a single PR to declare double-session readiness. Unknown history routes to conservative familiarization or clarification, not assumed advanced status.

### Routing and dose

| Condition / goal | Select and explain | Progression and scheduling rule |
|---|---|---|
| New/returning distance runner | Consistent easy run/walk, tolerable frequency, technique and basic strength. Initially omit maximal intervals and doubles | Establish repeatable tolerated work before intensity; no automatic weekly mileage percentage rule |
| Established runner improving 5–10 km aerobic power | A suitable longer-interval template such as R1, scaled to experience; explain that scaling is coaching inference | **Inference example:** begin with fewer bouts rather than imposing R1's full dose/frequency; add work only after repeatable completion and normal recovery |
| Established runner targeting sustainable pace / half-marathon or marathon | Controlled threshold-type intervals plus event-specific endurance, with an explicit anchor; strength may support durability | **Inference example:** 3 × 6–8 min with 1–2 min easy recovery is a conservative template choice, not a uniquely proven dose. Pace remains controlled; do not turn the last rep into a race |
| Trained cyclist seeking aerobic power | Choose C1 long intervals or, for suitable advanced riders, C2 short intervals; explain different evidence populations | Do not combine the full frequencies of both trials. Select a block and assess power, completion and recovery before another load change |
| Track sprint: acceleration | Fully rested short acceleration repetitions with technique and timed-quality focus | **Inference example:** 4–6 × 10–20 m for a technically prepared developmental athlete, full recovery; dose is not validated by an endurance SIT study |
| Track sprint: maximal speed | Flying sprints and sufficient run-in, safe surface and qualified technical input; use source P/best-practice ranges | Preserve quality; stop if mechanics degrade or speed cannot be reproduced. Do not shorten recovery to make it feel harder |
| Track sprint: 200/400 m speed endurance | Coach-led event-specific high-speed exposure after a base of safe sprinting | Do not extrapolate R1/C2 intervals or threshold doubles into a 400-m training plan. Exact work/rest must consider event, speed, tissue tolerance and phase |
| Beginner swimmer | Technique, water safety, breathing, short repeatable swims with adequate recovery | No maximal testing or fatigue-driven open-water training alone; no breath-holding challenges |
| Trained pool swimmer | Stroke/event-specific pacing and repeatability; distinguish sprint quality with long rest from aerobic/CSS sets | **Inference example:** 6–10 × 100 m near individual CSS with 15–30 s rest can be a template, but dose must be reduced for ability and technique; not established by S1 as optimal |
| Triathlon | Choose priorities across swim/bike/run; easy technical doubles may solve scheduling; some bricks support transition practice | Count the combined load. One hard bike plus one hard run is two quality exposures, even on different logs; no per-sport hard-session quota |
| HYROX | Build running capacity, station technique/strength, then partial run–station combinations matched to actual event rules | **Inference example:** a small set of controlled run–station repeats before longer combinations; do not mandate weekly all-out race simulations. Increase one station/distance/load component at a time |
| Supporting resistance | Technique first; then progressively heavier, event-relevant lower-body work plus appropriate upper-body/trunk work | Established lifters can use ST1/ST2-style concepts. No compulsory failure training. Preserve key endurance/sprint quality; reduce sets before removing all strength during busy phases |

**Frequency guardrail:** “One or two quality endurance sessions per week for many established recreational athletes” is a conservative coaching starting framework, not a biological law or an RCT-defined optimum. Some novice weeks need none; some qualified advanced blocks use more. Long runs with hard sections, races, demanding HYROX combinations and heavy unfamiliar lifting consume recovery capacity and must not be invisible additions. Schedule from the athlete's existing tolerated pattern rather than stacking every useful protocol into one week.

### Double-session decision tree

1. If a second session is required only to satisfy a weekly quota, remove it. No source here establishes a mandatory quota.
2. Classify proposal: easy+easy, technique+endurance, endurance+strength, easy+quality, or double-threshold. Never relabel two maximal sessions as double-threshold.
3. If novice/returning, unknown load history, current injury/illness, marked fatigue, inadequate time to refuel/rest, or uncalibrated effort control: do not auto-prescribe double-threshold. Offer one controlled session or an easy technical alternative.
4. If experienced, consistently tolerating existing quality work, goal-relevant, voluntarily interested, and able to recover: discuss an optional supervised experiment. Start by redistributing an already tolerated day's work rather than adding another full dose. This is conservative coaching inference from D1, not proven superior chronic training.
5. D1 used 6.5 h separation. That exact gap is a tested condition, not an established minimum. For endurance+strength, the ≥3 h subgroup in the concurrent meta-analysis likewise is not a guaranteed sufficient recovery period.
6. Recheck between sessions. Higher-than-usual exertion, worsening pain, poor technique, systemic illness, or failure to recover cancels/downsizes session two. There is no “make it up tomorrow” penalty.
7. Evaluate over a goal-relevant block using completion, perceived recovery and sport performance; do not use lower lactate alone to claim effectiveness. No automatic advancement from one double day to two.

### Progression and periodization

- Explicitly prioritize one outcome per block: consistency, aerobic volume, threshold endurance, aerobic power, maximal speed, race-specific integration, or taper. Maintain other abilities with lower doses where feasible.
- Change one major lever at a time as a **coaching risk-control inference**: repetitions/work duration, intensity, frequency, recovery density, station load or complexity. Do not increase all after one good session.
- Progress only after the existing work is repeatedly completed with intended effort/technique and expected recovery. There is no evidence-validated universal “two successes” or “10% per week” trigger; if software uses either operationally, label it a configurable heuristic rather than safety proof.
- When performance falls while effort rises, do not reflexively increase intensity. Consider accumulated training/life stress, illness, sleep, heat and fueling; reduce or hold load and seek qualified assessment for persistent unexplained decline.
- Use unloading and taper based on response, training history and event. R3's 3+1 pattern is one tested organization; not every athlete needs the same fourth-week reduction.
- Preserve specificity nearer competition without adding it on top of every previous stimulus. Marathon/long-course athletes need durable pacing and fueling practice; 100-m sprinters need speed quality; HYROX athletes need station transitions. These are training-principle applications, not identical trial-proven periodization algorithms.

## Stop conditions, referral gates and motivational behavior

These are general product safety gates, not diagnosis or individual medical clearance. Emergency-type symptoms such as chest pressure, fainting, severe unusual breathlessness, confusion or collapse require stopping exercise and urgent local help. Acute sharp pain, inability to bear weight, new marked swelling, or rapidly worsening pain requires ending the session and assessment rather than motivational escalation. Fever/systemic illness, recurrent bone pain, sustained unexplained performance loss, persistent fatigue, menstrual disruption or other under-fueling concerns block automatic intensity increases and prompt appropriate clinical/sport-professional support. A single watch readiness/HRV score must not diagnose illness or overtraining.

Technical stop conditions: altered running gait, loss of safe lifting mechanics, sprint mechanics deterioration, inability to maintain safe swimming technique, unsafe traffic/terrain, and heat/cold/water conditions that make the planned effort inappropriate. Stop/reduce even if target volume is unfinished. A session completed safely below plan is not failure.

Useful challenge language: “Aim for the planned pace with clean form; finish with enough control to repeat the session.” “The goal today is consistent reps, not the fastest final rep.” “You're showing up; keeping this easy protects the next quality session.” Avoid “no excuses,” shame, pain-as-proof, punishment sessions, compulsory fasting, compensatory exercise, or praising disregard of symptoms. Ask whether the user wants firm encouragement; preserve their choice to stop.

## Evidence gaps and release requirements

- This search found emerging 2026 double-threshold work but did not verify a peer-reviewed long-term trial proving superiority or universal weekly eligibility. Do not state that no such study could exist; state that it was not verified in this review.
- Female, older/master, novice, injured and para-athlete populations are underrepresented in many selected intervention studies. Do not convert absence of evidence into a blanket exclusion, or imply equal certainty in all groups.
- HYROX-specific longitudinal programming remains insufficiently established here. Correlations, race norms and single-athlete cases cannot supply a validated interval dose or periodization algorithm.
- A 2026 official HYROX pacing study and world-champion case study surfaced, but were not fully extracted in this pass; exclude numerical recommendations based on them until verified. Candidate records: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42697540/ and https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12662-026-01162-0.
- Some matrix entries are abstract-level verification. Before turning an exact research protocol into a deployable workout, retrieve full methods for warm-up, work intensity, recoveries, progression, adherence, exclusions and adverse events. Never fill missing protocol fields with guessed study details.
- Require test cases that reject zone-model mismatches, FTP=CP assumptions, acute→chronic claims, all-sports double quotas, race-improvement percentages copied from exhaustion tests, and motivational overrides of symptoms.

## Registry adjudications and motivation evidence

### Replace disputed generalized claims

- `HYPR_VOLUME_OPTIMUM`: Replace “10–20 sets maximizes hypertrophy” with “Higher weekly set volume can produce larger average hypertrophy gains, with diminishing returns and individual variation; no universally optimal 10–20-set interval is established.” Schoenfeld et al. 2017 analyzed 15 studies using categories <5, 5–9 and 10+ sets, not a direct validation of a 10–20 optimum. [PubMed; DOI 10.1080/02640414.2016.1210197](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27433992/). Pelland et al. 2026 (online Dec 2025) included 67 studies/2,058 participants (79.1% male); models distinguish direct/indirect sets and show diminishing returns. This is meta-regression, not proof of an individual optimal dose. Supporting strength for endurance must not inherit a bodybuilding volume quota. [PubMed; DOI 10.1007/s40279-025-02344-w](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41343037/).
- `CONCURRENT_SEPARATION`: Use the concurrent synthesis already cited above: same-session explosive-strength attenuation was greater than in the ≥3 h subgroup. No universal >6 h minimum follows from that source. Prioritize the key session, fatigue management and feasible separation; “3–6 h” may be a practical planning preference only if explicitly labeled as such.
- `TAPER_OPTIMUM`: Replace “maximizes” with “a commonly supported starting strategy in competitive endurance athletes, individualized to event, prior load and response.” Do not export the pooled taper as mandatory in novice, sprint, HYROX or injury-return plans.
- `POLARIZED_SUPERIORITY`: Replace universal “80/20 is superior” with “predominantly easy training with targeted quality is well supported; polarized and pyramidal arrangements can both work, and comparative superiority depends on population, measurement and comparator.” R2, T1 and C3 provide direct reasons to reject a universal superiority claim.
- `ZONE_EQUIVALENCE`: Replace synonym mappings with named anchors, provenance and uncertain crosswalks. A conventional percentage zone is not an individual physiological threshold test.

### Motivation/adherence: evidence separate from original cues

Silva et al. 2010 randomized 239 women in a one-year weight-management program to an autonomy-supportive intervention versus general health education. Physical activity and autonomous motivation improved. This supports offering choice, meaningful goals and competence-building; it does not validate a particular chatbot phrase, compulsory athlete training dose, or coercive “push.” [Primary study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20012179/).

Nuss et al. 2023 tested motivational interviewing, a wearable tracker, both, or education in 40 insufficiently active adults over 12 weeks. Motivational outcomes improved in some intervention conditions without clear improvement in activity participation. This is a useful warning that motivation-score gains do not guarantee adherence. [Primary study DOI 10.1080/02640414.2023.2195228](https://doi.org/10.1080/02640414.2023.2195228); [PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36966352/). Sex split was not extracted; do not invent it.

Implementation inference: request preferred encouragement style, choose one achievable process goal tied to the actual session, offer a smaller safe alternative after barriers, acknowledge completion/recovery choices, and track actual adherence separately from expressed motivation. No retrieved study validates overriding pain or fatigue to improve adherence.

## Candidate rule IDs and implementation contract

These are proposed stable IDs for review, not a claim that runtime integration is complete. Every record should contain `source_ids`, `evidence_type`, `source_population`, `target_population`, `directness`, `observed_protocol`, `coaching_adaptation`, `missing_data_gate`, `stop_conditions`, `unsupported_extrapolations`, `reviewed_at`, `status`, and `version`. ### Legacy boxing scope boundary

The repository audit also identified a legacy boxing generator. Boxing-specific conditioning, technical training, sparring load, head-impact exposure, medical clearance and return-to-contact protocols were **not reviewed in this evidence pass**. Do not label the legacy generator research-validated, transfer cycling/running HIIT doses into sparring, or silently include boxing in an “all sports reviewed” claim. Candidate gate `TRN-BOXING-LEGACY-UNREVIEWED`: `status=needs_review`, `numeric_protocol=null`, `auto_prescription=false` until its intended scope and qualified sport-specific review are completed. ## 10 October implementation review: cycles, pace and practical examples

This is an addendum to the existing dossier, not a separate evidence search or a claim of qualified individual coaching approval. The reproducible [12-week example pack](reviewed-cycle-examples.md) covers all nine currently accepted planning goals: four triathlon distances, distance running, track sprint, cycling, swimming and HYROX. General strength is included as **partial supporting scope**, not a newly supported primary goal. “Olympic” in the goal enum means Olympic-distance triathlon; standalone Olympic lifting and legacy boxing are excluded. All people, results, events and training histories in the pack are explicitly synthetic examples.

### Clarifications checked against primary sources

- **C1 / Seiler:** retain the original 35 recreational cyclists, seven weeks and maximal-tolerable-session-intensity context. The 4 × 8-minute result is not a universal optimum and the pooled physiological change is not a race-time improvement. The sample pack's controlled 3 × 4 minutes is an explicitly different JMM coaching choice, not a replication. [Primary publisher record](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1600-0838.2011.01351.x); [PMID 21812820](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21812820/).
- **C2 / elite 30/15:** full-method verification on 10 October confirms 18 male national-level cyclists in a three-week block after substantial prior training. Short intervals improved selected performance outcomes compared with that particular long-interval comparator; VO2max change did not differ between groups. Remove the legacy universal “about twice the gain” / winner claim. The tested 3 × (13 × 30 s / 15 s), with 3 min between sets, must not be conflated with arbitrarily filling a workout's remaining minutes or with running. [Primary full article, methods and results](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/sms.13627); [PMID 31977120](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31977120/).
- **Z2 / additional FTP–CP check:** McGrath et al. 2021 found CP and FTP non-interchangeable in highly trained cyclists/triathletes. A group-average bias or a proposed sample-specific correction cannot safely become an individual crosswalk. Keep protocol, source and separate fields. [Primary study, PMID 34055164](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34055164/). This supplements Z1 above.
- **S2 / CSS:** preserve same-stroke, pool/start conditions and measurement uncertainty from the original review. A synthetic 400 m time of 480 s and 200 m time of 220 s yields `(480−220)/2 = 130 s/100 m`; the arithmetic does not establish LT2, open-water readiness or eligibility for a maximal test. [Primary study, PMID 38380294](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38380294/).
- **Doctor/expert material:** reuse the existing [Huberman–Galpin adjudication](huberman-galpin-evidence.md) and `huberman-galpin-claims.json`. Episode teaching and mnemonics remain expert education, distinct from RCTs, syntheses and individual medical advice. No doctor's name validates JMM doses, recovery cutoffs or event-priority rules.

### Pace units and authoritative execution model

`buildZoneTable` describes running **seconds per kilometre** reference bands. Its multipliers act on pace time: an easy multiplier greater than 1 makes the number larger and the running slower. Dividing inverted the physiology. For a 300 s/km reference, Z1 is 375–480 s/km and Z2 is 330–375 s/km. Swimming retains its own seconds-per-100 m model; do not apply running factors to CSS.

`canonical-session.resolveStepTarget` is the executable step authority. Its representative running multipliers (1.4, 1.2, 1.08, 1, 0.95, 0.9, 0.85) sit within the corrected table bands. They are coaching references, not measured physiological domains. Explicit reviewed step targets take precedence, including open effort. Missing or expired evidence must not be manufactured from a goal, repeated observation, another sport or a source citation. The existing athlete-opted-in 5 km-derived threshold estimate must remain labeled as an estimate with its derivation; raw 5 km performance is not directly measured threshold.

Cross-path regression tests cover table ordering, easy-versus-threshold direction, canonical references, legacy display/distance math and swim separation. The legacy interval selector still exists for previously supported workouts; removing its superiority language does not make all its recipes validated. New cycle examples never activate that selector.

### Review-only dose and schedule contract

- Every sample dose, rest interval, schedule, progression opportunity, A/B/C priority handling and 28-day review window is a **JMM coaching heuristic**. The 90-day anchor expiry is a product validity rule, not a biological guarantee.
- The samples show all 12 weeks, session-by-session work/rest, exact endpoint sums, baseline examples, reduced weeks, an event day and a post-event review week. They are not automatically selectable in production. Qualified coaching approval is explicitly absent.
- Structured endpoints are editable. Recompute from the steps after edits: do not preserve a stale summary. Distances remain distances and elapsed time remains unknown until performed; a session time budget is not an exact swim/sprint duration. Rest occurs between repetitions, not after the last by accident.
- Foundation and review observations are non-maximal and replace existing time, never add testing load. Later weeks remain conditional on actual work, effort, technical quality and recovery. A review reminder does not renew an anchor or imply progression is safe.
- Event importance is distinct from physiological cost. Protect the actual event date, reduce rather than stack training and request a decision for close events. Race duration, completion and recovery are unknown. The sample post-event week deliberately has no prescribed workouts until actual response is reviewed.
- Limited general-strength examples contain familiar technique windows, explicitly reviewed exercise/load/repetition choices and real rest. They do not claim a 1RM program, prescribe Olympic lifts, or smuggle mechanical demand under an easy HR label.

The evidence matrix and source IDs remain the source of truth. The example pack, typed fixtures and regression tests are implementation review artifacts; no real athlete records were changed to produce them.
