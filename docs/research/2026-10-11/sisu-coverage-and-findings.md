## Claims that cannot enter prescription logic unchanged

1. The double-threshold article treats a narrow lactate range and an approximate HR percentage as broadly applicable threshold anchors. JMM needs the athlete’s sport, test method and individual response. LT1, LT2, critical speed, FTP and maximal lactate steady state are not interchangeable.
2. The articles’ strong recovery/burnout language is not established by the identified evidence. Reduced lactate or an elapsed gap is not proof of tissue recovery, adequate fuel, or immunity from overtraining.
3. The actual [Talsnes crossover](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2024.1428536/full) tested acute responses in trained men using uphill treadmill work, with total workload matching. It did not test long-term race gains from obligatory double-threshold days.
4. Neither article establishes an optimal four-day cycle for every athlete. Keep individual scheduling, total cross-sport load, symptoms and recovery review.
5. Source prominence is not evidence quality. Sisu material can supply ideas to investigate; it cannot fill absent numeric fields or override better controlled human evidence.
